1 What a barcode really is

A barcode (the black lines on a product) is just a number. That's all. It does not hold the name, price, or expiry. It is only an ID — like a phone number.

📱 Think of your phone contacts. A phone number alone is just digits. You save it once with a name — "Ramesh" — and forever after, your phone shows "Ramesh" when he calls. Our system works the exact same way.

2 You teach each product ONCE

The first time the computer ever sees a product, it doesn't know it. You teach it once — link the barcode to the product name. Like saving a new contact. After that, it's remembered forever.

You teach one time per product — never per bottle, never per delivery. Teach it on day one, and you never teach that product again.

3 The same product is known forever

Every Diet Coke in the world has the same barcode. The 1st, the 2nd, the 1000th — identical. So once you teach the 1st one, every Diet Coke after that is recognised instantly.

1st Diet Coke → "don't know it" → you teach it once ✅ 2nd Diet Coke → same barcode! → "Ah, Diet Coke" → done ✅ 3rd, 4th, 100th → just scan → done ✅
You do NOT re-teach the same product. Same barcode = recognised automatically, like Ramesh calling again — the phone already knows him.

4 Where the details come from (name, price, unit)

The details live inside the product, not in the barcode. When the barcode is linked, scanning shows them — because they were already saved. And good news: your 523 products are already loaded with their details (name, category, unit, price) from your store's stock file.

5 Expiry dates & batches (the important one)

The expiry date is printed separately on the pack — it is not inside the small bottle barcode. So the barcode stays the same, but each delivery can have a different expiry. The system handles this with batches:

Diet Coke — 1st delivery: 20 bottles, expiry Dec 2026 → Batch #1 Diet Coke — 2nd delivery: 30 bottles, expiry Mar 2027 → Batch #2 ▲ same barcode ▲ different expiry, kept separate

Each delivery becomes its own batch with its own expiry. When you sell, the system gives out the oldest one first (Batch #1) — so nothing expires sitting on the shelf. That's called FIFO (First In, First Out).

For loose bottles, you type the expiry once per delivery (same date for the whole lot). If goods come in a wholesale carton with a long barcode, that barcode holds the expiry inside — and it fills in automatically.

🔁 The same product arrives again (next time)

1
Press green INWARD. Type how many. Scan.
✅ Recognised instantly. No list. Done.

1
Press orange OUTWARD. Type how many. Scan.
✅ Stock removed (oldest batch first). Done.
